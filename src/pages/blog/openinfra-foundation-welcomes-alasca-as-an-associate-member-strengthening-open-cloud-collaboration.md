The OpenInfra Foundation is elated to be joining [ALASCA](https://alasca.cloud/en/), the Association for Operational, Open Cloud Infrastructures, as an Associate Member, strengthening collaboration between two communities committed to open, interoperable, and vendor-neutral infrastructure.

Founded in Germany in 2022, ALASCA brings together organizations and developers advancing operational open source cloud technologies. Its mission aligns closely with the OpenInfra Foundation's commitment to giving organizations greater choice and control over their infrastructure.

As demand for digital sovereignty grows, collaboration across open source communities is increasingly important.

## Shared Priorities for Open Infrastructure

ALASCA and OpenInfra share a common vision: organizations should have the freedom to deploy and manage their infrastructure without depending on proprietary technologies or individual vendors.

The OpenInfra Foundation supports this mission through projects including OpenStack, Kata Containers, StarlingX, and Zuul. ALASCA complements these technologies through projects focused on simplifying cloud deployment and operations.

One example is Yaook, an ALASCA project that automates OpenStack deployment and lifecycle management using Kubernetes. Other projects, including Krake, Tarook, and Arko, address challenges in workload orchestration, Kubernetes management, and cloud operations.

Together, these technologies help organizations build and operate production-ready open source infrastructure.

## Collaboration in Action at the ALASCA Summit

The communities' shared priorities were on display at the [ALASCA Summit 2026](https://alasca.cloud/en/alasca-summit-2026/), held November 3–4 in Dresden, Germany.

For the first time, the event featured a dedicated OpenInfra Track, bringing together developers, operators, and community members to exchange practical experience with open infrastructure technologies.

Sessions explored OpenStack security and identity management, automated VM migration using Yaook, alternatives to hyperscale cloud providers, and strategies for migrating from VMware to OpenStack. The track also examined emerging AI infrastructure requirements, including sandboxing AI agents and delivering sovereign AI services on OpenStack.

The OpenInfra Track demonstrated how the two communities are already working together to address shared technical challenges and advance open source infrastructure adoption.

## Advancing Digital Sovereignty Together

Digital sovereignty has become a strategic priority for governments, enterprises, and service providers seeking greater control over their data, workloads, and infrastructure.

Achieving that independence requires more than data residency. It depends on open technologies, interoperability, transparent development, and the ability to operate infrastructure without vendor lock-in.

“ALASCA and OpenInfra share a commitment to open, vendor-neutral infrastructure. As demand for digital sovereignty grows, our collaboration will strengthen open source cloud technologies and give organizations greater choice and control over their infrastructure,” said Thierry Carrez, General Manager of the OpenInfra Foundation.

By connecting ALASCA's European community and operational expertise with OpenInfra's global ecosystem, both organizations can expand awareness, adoption, and contributions to open infrastructure technologies.

## Building on a Shared Commitment

The OpenInfra Track at the ALASCA Summit is one example of how collaboration across communities can translate shared priorities into practical progress.

As an Associate Member, ALASCA strengthens the connection between communities working to make open source infrastructure more accessible, operationally sustainable, and widely adopted.

The OpenInfra Foundation looks forward to building on this collaboration to advance open infrastructure and digital sovereignty worldwide.

Learn more about [ALASCA](https://alasca.cloud/en/) and the [OpenInfra Foundation](https://openinfra.org/).
